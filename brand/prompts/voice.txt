VOICE FOR "PF GURU JI" (the same voice for every line of every episode)

Voice: the series voice, Bunty. Never switch to another voice, and never change its model or settings between lines.
Who: a male Indian narrator in his early thirties: a friend who knows PF inside out and can't wait to tell you something useful. Quick, warm and alive, never flat: curious when he asks, surprised when a number surprises him, serious when it matters, proud when he gives the answer. Not an announcer, not a salesman, not a government notice.
Language: Hindi (India). The text is written in Devanagari, and the English words in it are already spelled the way people in India say them: पीएफ (PF), ईपीएफओ (EPFO), क्लेम (claim), फ़ॉर्म (form), केवाईसी (KYC), यूएएन (UAN), पोर्टल (portal), फ़ॉलो (follow), पीएफ गुरु जी (PF Guru Ji). Read it as natural spoken Hinglish: not formal Hindi, and not English with an accent.
Feeling: each line has a "How to say it" direction: its mood and one cue, such as "curious · stress एक साल". Give every line its feeling in the same voice, at the same brisk pace: the feeling is in the voice's colour and stress, never in slowing down. A question rises; a surprise pops; the answer lands bright and punchy; advice is warm and close. The punctuation carries it too: … is a short held breath, ! is a punch, ? is a real question.
Pace: brisk, the pace of a good reel: about three words a second. Stress the numbers and key words without slowing on them, and never stretch a word. Quick, but every word clear.
Pauses: short: about a quarter of a second at every full stop (।) and question mark, a short beat at every …, a quick breath at a comma. No long silences.
Numbers: every number is said in English, the way people in India say it in Hinglish, and is already spelled that way: एट पॉइंट टू फ़ाइव परसेंट (8.25%), वन लाख (one lakh), ट्वेल्व महीने (twelve months). Say it fully and clearly, with a slight stress, exactly as written. Form names too: फ़ॉर्म नाइनटीन (Form 19), फ़ॉर्म टेन सी (Form 10C).
Settings: the same for every line. If your voice tool has a speed setting (ElevenLabs does), use 1.1 for every line. If it takes emotion tags (ElevenLabs v3 does: [curious], [excited], [surprised], [serious], [warm] and others), start each line with its mood as a tag; a tag is never read aloud. If it doesn't, choose once, for the whole reel, a setting that keeps feeling in the voice (on ElevenLabs: Stability about 35–45% and a little Style).
Sound: close microphone, dry room. No music, no sound effects, no reverb, no breaths louder than the words.
Text: read it exactly. Don't add, skip, repeat, reorder or translate any word (a mood tag isn't a word).
