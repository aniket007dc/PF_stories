Rules:
1. 150–175 words in total (about 60–70 seconds at a brisk pace). Sentences of 3–9 words.
2. One line per shot, 14–18 shots, in this chapter order (the story's beats): hook, problem, why, steps, check, help, share, signoff.
   - hook (max 7 s): the hero's moment and the twist, in a line or two ("Shivam ne nayi naukri join ki. Pehle hi din, ek chhoti si galti.").
   - problem (max 10 s): what it costs the hero: money stuck, a claim back, months lost.
   - why (max 20 s): why it happened, only as EPFO's own rules say: someone who knows (Priya from HR, or Ravi) says it, or the narrator counts "Ek: … Do: …".
   - steps (max 25 s): what the hero does, in order, naming the real screens, menus and forms ("Member portal, Manage, phir KYC").
   - check (max 12 s): the one thing the hero checks, and what skipping it would have cost.
   - help (max 10 s): where the hero goes if it's still stuck: an official channel from the data sheet.
   - share: one line that ends "… abhi bhejo.", aimed at one kind of friend.
   - signoff: exactly "{{SIGNOFF}}"
3. The first time an assumption from the sheet is used, that line must say "maan lo".
4. EPFO rules:
   - Say only what EPFO's own pages, circulars and forms say, through the data sheet. Name a form only from the list above, and only for what it's for.
   - Never ask viewers for their UAN, password, OTP, Aadhaar, PAN or bank details: not in the reel, the caption or the pinned comment. The pinned comment may ask what went wrong, never for personal details.
   - Never promise an outcome or a date. A time limit is EPFO's rule from the data sheet, not a guarantee.
   - Send people only to official channels named in the data sheet: the member portal, the UMANG app, their EPFO office, the grievance portal. Never to an agent, a paid service, or a phone number that isn't in the sheet.
   - A phone number goes on a card, never in the voice line.
5. In "vo" (shown on screen as the captions), write Roman-script Hinglish, with numbers as digits, the ₹ sign and Indian commas (₹50,000, 1,00,000). Write forms as "Form 19", "Form 10C".
   In "tts" (read by the voice), write the same words in Devanagari, the script a Hindi voice reads correctly: every number said in English, the way people say numbers in Hinglish, and spelled in Devanagari (एट पॉइंट टू फ़ाइव परसेंट for 8.25%, वन लाख रुपीज़ for ₹1,00,000, एट थाउज़ेंड टू हंड्रेड फ़िफ़्टी for ₹8,250, ट्वेल्व महीने for 12 mahine; keep लाख and करोड़), and every English word spelled the way people in India say it (पीएफ, ईपीएफओ, क्लेम, फ़ॉर्म, केवाईसी, यूएएन, पोर्टल, फ़ॉलो, पीएफ गुरु जी). Say form names in English: Form 19 is फ़ॉर्म नाइनटीन, Form 10C is फ़ॉर्म टेन सी, Form 31 is फ़ॉर्म थर्टी वन. No digits, and no words that aren't in "vo". Use । for full stops, and let the punctuation carry the feeling: … for a held breath before a reveal, ! for a punch, ? for a real question.
